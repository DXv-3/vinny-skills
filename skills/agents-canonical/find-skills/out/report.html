  • [Deliberation Structures] FISHBOWL variants: open (any observer joins anytime), closed (fixed inner group, rotate on a timer), tap-out (observer taps an inner agent to swap), silent (outer circle only observes and annotates). HIGH
  • [Swarm Simulation (MiroFish/OASIS)] God's-eye variable injection: dynamically inject variables mid-simulation to deduce alternative future trajectories ('what-if' rehearsal at zero real-world risk). HIGH

  • [Swarm Simulation (MiroFish/OASIS)] Deep interaction: chat with any individual agent in the simulated world, or with the ReportAgent, to interrogate why an outcome emerged. HIGH

prompt (5)

  • [Co-STORM (Stanford/Yale)] Multi-agent roles: instantiate perspective-A agent, perspective-B agent, and a Moderator that steers and injects complementary information. HIGH
  • [Brainstorm Layer (custom)] Stanford academic framing: rewrite every surviving idea as a research question and flag its citation needs, so STORM's grounded-writing stage has concrete claims to ground. HIGH
  • [Deliberation Structures] COUNCIL roles: N peer members + 1 chairman; optionally assign adversarial roles to peers to prevent echo chambers. HIGH
  • [Deliberation Structures] DEBATE roles: proponent, opponent, and (optional) judge; assign adversarial personas to force genuine opposition and surface the premise left unsaid. HIGH
  • [Deliberation Structures] FISHBOWL fit: it lives in the DIVERGENT phase (Kaner's Diamond) — surface many perspectives before converging; pairs naturally with the Brainstorm Layer's perspective explosion. HIGH

workflow (19)

  • [STORM (Stanford OVAL)] Perspective discovery: survey existing articles on similar topics to enumerate diverse viewpoints before asking questions. HIGH
  • [STORM (Stanford OVAL)] Perspective-guided question asking: each discovered persona asks questions from its own angle (beats naive 'ask 30 questions'). HIGH
  • [STORM (Stanford OVAL)] Simulated conversation: a writer-persona converses with a topic-expert grounded on trusted internet sources, updating understanding and asking follow-ups. HIGH
  • [STORM (Stanford OVAL)] Curation to outline: synthesize the collected Q&A into a structured outline (the core pre-writing artifact). HIGH
  • [STORM (Stanford OVAL)] Grounded writing stage: expand the outline into a full article with inline citations to the retrieved references. HIGH
  • [Co-STORM (Stanford/Yale)] Collaborative discourse protocol: a turn-management policy decides which agent speaks next to keep the multi-agent conversation productive. HIGH
  • [Co-STORM (Stanford/Yale)] Human-in-the-loop steering: agents ask questions on the user's behalf; the user mostly observes and can inject direction at any turn. HIGH
  • [Brainstorm Layer (custom)] Problem reframing: restate the prompt 5 ways before ideating — invert it, generalize it, tightly constrain it, analogize it to another domain, and reduce it to first principles. HIGH
  • [Brainstorm Layer (custom)] Perspective explosion: generate wildly diverse personas beyond Co-STORM's expert A/B — adversary, novice, historian, futurist, contrarian, cross-domain outsider. These feed STORM's perspective-discovery step. HIGH
  • [Brainstorm Layer (custom)] Divergent idea storm: run SCAMPER + random-stimulus + 'worst possible idea' passes to force quantity and novelty. No filtering yet — quantity first. HIGH
  • [Brainstorm Layer (custom)] Unknown-unknown probes: invert Co-STORM's mind map — explicitly ask 'what would we NOT think to ask?' to seed the multi-agent discourse with blind spots. HIGH
  • [Brainstorm Layer (custom)] Convergence gate: cluster and dedupe ideas, then score each by novelty × feasibility × fit; only survivors pass to STORM as perspectives + seed questions. HIGH
  • [Deliberation Structures] COUNCIL (3-stage): each agent answers the prompt independently, then anonymously ranks the other answers, then a chairman synthesizes one final response from the ranked set. HIGH
  • [Deliberation Structures] JURY/PANEL: 3+ judge agents independently evaluate, surface disagreement, run deliberation rounds toward consensus; compare jury accuracy vs a single judge. HIGH
  • [Deliberation Structures] DEBATE (MAD): 2+ debaters take positions and argue over N rounds with access to debate history; resolve via an LLM judge or self-consistency majority vote. HIGH
  • [Deliberation Structures] FISHBOWL turn policy: seed the inner circle with a few agents, keep 1-2 empty chairs; an outer-circle agent may take an empty chair (an inner agent then rotates out), continuously refreshing viewpoints. HIGH
  • [Swarm Simulation (MiroFish/OASIS)] Seed -> digital world: extract seed information (news, policy drafts, financial signals, or a story) and auto-construct a high-fidelity parallel world to run the crowd inside. HIGH
  • [Swarm Simulation (MiroFish/OASIS)] Graph building: build a GraphRAG knowledge graph with individual + collective memory injection, then dynamic temporal memory updates as the simulation runs. HIGH
  • [Swarm Simulation (MiroFish/OASIS)] Environment setup: extract entity relationships from the seed, auto-generate agent personas, and inject per-agent configuration before simulating. HIGH

table (3)

  • [STORM (Stanford OVAL)] Eval via FreshWiki dataset + rubric metrics: ROUGE, entity recall, heading entity recall, outline quality. HIGH
  • [Co-STORM (Stanford/Yale)] Eval via WildSeek dataset (real info-seeking goals); 70% of participants prefer Co-STORM over a search engine, 78% over a RAG chatbot. HIGH
  • [Deliberation Structures] JURY voting rules: majority (ties default to reject/low-confidence), unanimous (accept only if all concur), or reliability-weighted aggregation (weight each juror by an estimated reliability score). HIGH

snippet (0)

mental-model (7)

  • [STORM (Stanford OVAL)] STORM = Synthesis of Topic Outlines through Retrieval and Multi-perspective Question Asking; two stages: pre-writing (research+outline) then writing (grounded article with citations). HIGH
  • [Co-STORM (Stanford/Yale)] Co-STORM = collaborative discourse among several LM agents that the user observes and occasionally steers, surfacing 'unknown unknowns' the user wouldn't think to ask. HIGH
  • [Brainstorm Layer (custom)] BRAINSTORM is the DIVERGENT front-end to STORM/Co-STORM: open the problem space wide before STORM converges. It outputs exactly what STORM consumes — perspectives + seed questions — so the two stages compose. HIGH
  • [Deliberation Structures] Deliberation structures are pluggable discourse formats that sit where Co-STORM's plain multi-agent conversation runs; pick the format to match the decision (converge vs diverge, judge vs consensus). HIGH
  • [Deliberation Structures] FISHBOWL: a small inner circle actively discusses while a larger outer circle observes; an empty chair lets an observer swap in, so perspectives rotate through the conversation. HIGH
  • [Swarm Simulation (MiroFish/OASIS)] SWARM SIMULATION is the largest deliberation scale: instead of a few debating agents, run thousands of persona-agents with independent personalities, long-term memory, and behavioral logic that interact and socially evolve to reveal EMERGENT outcomes. HIGH
  • [Swarm Simulation (MiroFish/OASIS)] Use-tier selector: pick swarm simulation for EMERGENT crowd outcomes/prediction; pick council/jury/panel/debate for a VERDICT from a few experts; pick fishbowl/brainstorm for DIVERGENT perspective surfacing. HIGH

edge-case (6)

  • [STORM (Stanford OVAL)] Known failure modes: source bias transfer and over-association of unrelated facts; guard against both when curating. HIGH
  • [Co-STORM (Stanford/Yale)] Limitations: higher latency (utterance-intent decision + mind-map update), weak tailoring to user prior knowledge, English-centric. HIGH
  • [Brainstorm Layer (custom)] Handoff contract: BRAINSTORM diverges -> convergence gate filters -> STORM/Co-STORM grounds & writes. Never let divergent noise reach the writing stage unfiltered (avoids over-association of unrelated facts). HIGH
  • [Deliberation Structures] JURY caveat: correlate panel disagreement with question difficulty; use adaptive stopping (e.g., stability detection) so deliberation ends when consensus dynamics stabilize instead of running fixed rounds. HIGH
  • [Deliberation Structures] DEBATE caveat: LLMs anchor to initial positions ('hung jury' effect) and heavy RLHF alignment makes them rigid; explicitly prompt open-mindedness and prefer flexible models for mind-changing. HIGH
  • [Swarm Simulation (MiroFish/OASIS)] Swarm caveat: high token/compute cost — start with short runs (e.g. <40 rounds) before scaling; simulation fidelity depends on persona and memory quality. HIGH
